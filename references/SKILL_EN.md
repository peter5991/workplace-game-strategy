# Workplace Power-Game Strategy Analysis (English Version)

> This is the English-language version of the skill. It is loaded only when the user writes in a language other than Chinese (see the Language routing section at the top of `SKILL.md`). Respond entirely in the user's language.
>
> Note on jurisdiction: several concrete tools in this framework reference Chinese institutional and labor-law terrain (编制, labor arbitration, labor-inspector complaints). When the user is in another jurisdiction, keep the game-theoretic structure and swap in the local equivalents (e.g. HR policy text, grievance procedures, employment tribunals, small-claims channels) — and always tell the user to verify the actual local rules.

## When to use

The user has a structural conflict with a manager / supervisor / organization / employer: excessive task assignment, off-duty work dumped on them, withheld pay or bonuses, performance-review threats, credit or authorship theft, broken promises in quid-pro-quo exchanges, or the urge to apply external pressure. The goal is not to "win one conflict" but to improve the user's structural position in a long-term repeated game.

## Intake (mandatory before Step 0)

Every analysis in this framework rests on the user's **real leverage**. Starting without knowing the user's cards means fabricating leverage for them — and fabricated leverage guarantees a failed strategy. On trigger, first judge: has the user already made the following three things clear? If yes, go straight to Step 0. If not, ask the intake checklist **in one consolidated round** (one message, not drip-fed questions).

**Intake checklist (three questions):**

1. **The problem**: What specifically happened? What is the one thing you most want solved?
   - Prompt example: "Tell me about the single thing that bothers you most — the more concrete the better. 'My bonus was docked $300 this month, the reason given was…' is far more useful than 'my boss has it in for me.'"
2. **Situation context**: What kind of organization (public sector / private / foreign-owned), your role and contract status (tenure/establishment, fixed-term, probation), who the counterpart is and where their power comes from (scheduling / review / approval authority), how long the conflict has run, and whether you've clashed before.
3. **Hole-card inventory**: walk the user through a self-check, item by item — **never assume on their behalf**:
   - Scarce output: papers / projects / results / client resources / irreplaceable skills — is there anything that "no one else can do if you leave"?
   - Institutional identity: tenure/establishment, employment contract clauses, talent titles, formal credentials — which of these exist in writing?
   - Outside options: what are you worth on the market right now? Any other offers or paths (BATNA)?
   - Rule ammunition: written policies, clauses, or documents that favor you (the counterpart's verbal retelling doesn't count — original text only);
   - Evidence stockpile: pay stubs, attendance records, deduction notices, chat logs, emails — how much have you already saved?

**Special cases:**

- The user only wants to vent: empathize for two sentences first, then bridge back with "let me break this down and do the math for you";
- The user won't disclose private details: switch to the hypothetical method — "no problem, I'll play out both typical scenarios for you";
- The user claims to have "no cards at all": do not accept this conclusion — walk the full inventory before judging. Most people undercount rule ammunition and evidence stockpiles, which are exactly where the Type-B framework starts.

## Step 0: User classification — decides which framework to use (mandatory)

**Classification test: does the user hold "something the counterpart wants and cannot easily get"** (tenure/establishment, scarce output, external networks, irreplaceable skills)?

- **Type A: Resource holder** (high-level talent, research backbone, tenured or externally connected) → Part 1, the "six-module framework"; core leverage is **united-front value**;
- **Type B: Ordinary worker** (no united-front value, highly replaceable) → Part 2, the "worker framework"; core leverage is **exit rights + law as rule terrain**;
- Shared by both: counterpart-type diagnosis (Step 3.5), paper-trail discipline, and card-sequencing principles.

# Part 1: Type A (Resource holder) — six-module framework

## Step 1: Stratify the problems

Characterize each grievance:

- **Rule hardness**: black-and-white written clause > vague catch-all clause > purely verbal arrangement. The harder the clause, the stronger the card;
- **Severity**: distinguish "labor exploitation" (negotiable) from "principle risk" (academic misconduct, disciplinary violations, legal liability — draw the line, never trade);
- **Dependency logic**: identify "control loops" (busywork eats your time → output drops → review failure → deeper dependence on the counterpart). Loops must be broken before they close.

## Step 2: Assess the power position

- User's safety cushion: nature of tenure/establishment, review power structure (the counterpart's actual weight), contract term, outside options (BATNA);
- The counterpart's real weapons: list them one by one (scheduling power, certification power, review voice, etc.), and for each ask "what precondition does it need to work?" — most under-the-table moves depend on "vague rules + user silence + no evidence"; remove any one and the shell is a dud;
- The counterpart's pressure points and interests: performance indicators, evaluation pressure, obligations to their own superiors;
- **Discipline: read the original rule text before choosing a strategy.** Rules as relayed by the counterpart (e.g. "lab sessions don't count toward review") may themselves be part of their strategy.

## Step 3: Assemble the six modules

- **A — Compliance identity (foundation)**: apply for formal in-system status/credentials (entrepreneurship approval, position reclassification, talent certification) so time allocation gains official grounding. Technique: embed clauses favorable to the user in application materials — the counterpart's signature is tacit acceptance;
- **B — In-rules negotiation (middle)**: never refuse the total load, only negotiate its structure; offer multiple-choice, not yes-no (ABCD tiered proposals as face-saving ramps); unified narrative: "I want to ace my review, but my time structure won't let me"; package requests at natural milestones as a "work-plan briefing": report wins → show data → offer choices;
- **C — United-front value cultivation (top)**: a person's value depends on the network behind them. Inventory the user's connections, map them against the counterpart's most-needed performance indicators; deliver one visible value transfer and let the counterpart take the stage (the user pulled the resource; the credit is theirs). **Red line: transfer increments (introductions, platforms, channels) — never transfer stock (authorship, funding, ownership of results)**;
- **D — Credit-risk management**: never hand over irreversible benefits first; stage exchanges / simultaneous delivery, or counterpart delivers first; put verbal promises in writing; make fulfillment progress visible; test credit with small transactions before big ones; mentally reclassify "they broke their word" from a moral judgment to a risk parameter;
- **E — Vulnerability offense-defense**: two moves against under-the-table tactics — pin down rules beforehand (manufacture written confirmation at the moment the act occurs: confirmation = commitment, denial = evidence, silence is also evidence); pin down responsibility mid-act (written substitute requests + a "policy consultation" filing with a third-party department + full paper trail of diligent performance, core narrative: "my performance is flawless; every gap was created by the assigning side"); post-hoc remedies: evidence chain → written objection during the public-notice window → review → appeal; provable retaliation escalates it into a disciplinary violation by management;
- **F — Off-field hole card**: find growth channels outside the counterpart's jurisdiction (external credentials, market-recognized titles). The point is to remove the foundation of their power. Discipline: total secrecy until secured; once secured, let the counterpart do the math themselves. First check the contract for hard linkage clauses.

## Step 3.5: Counterpart-type diagnosis (decides the main module — mandatory first)

The default assumption is "rational but selfish, winnable." But **extreme counterparts** exist: pure extractors — short-sighted, planless. Diagnose before assembling:

- **Diagnostic — the small-stakes transaction test**: offer one low-cost cooperation opportunity and watch whether they honor it. Honored → standard assumption; not honored → extreme assumption. **Don't guess. Test.**
- Misdiagnosis costs are symmetric: misreading a winnable counterpart as an extractor = self-fulfilling enmity; misreading an extractor as winnable = paying several rounds of extortion rent before waking up.

**Adaptation under the extreme assumption (trunk intact, channels closed):**

| Module | Status |
|---|---|
| C United-front value | ❌ Fails. Beware the **extortion equilibrium**: tribute doesn't buy peace, it confirms "you have oil and you'll pay" — extortion reprices upward each round |
| D Credit risk | ✅ Upgraded to core defense |
| E Vulnerability offense-defense | ✅ Upgraded to main defense. Extractors lean harder on under-the-table moves, their behavior often already violates rules, evidence chains gain value |
| F Off-field hole card | ✅ Upgraded to main attack: the goal shifts from "make them treat me well" to "reduce their capacity to harm me to zero"; lower tolerance ceiling, earlier exit preparation |
| A/B | ⚠️ Effective with downgraded expectations: their power comes from "refusing an unimpeachable request leaves a procedural cost" |
| External-force discipline | 🔄 Lowered threshold: when the counterpart's behavior itself violates rules, reporting is legitimate remedy; still one decisive strike, no advance threats |

Meta-conclusion: of the six modules, only C depends on the counterpart's rationality; the rest are all built on the assumption "selfish and short-sighted." Under the extreme assumption, the master line becomes: **"Build the firewall and the exit channel, reduce their extraction capacity to zero; every transgression of theirs is just one more page in the evidence chain."**

# Part 2: Type B (Ordinary worker) framework

## Theoretical core

- **Power-dependence theory (Emerson)**: the boss's power = your dependence on the job ÷ your alternatives. Exploitation is rooted in structural dependence, not in the boss being bad;
- **Cost asymmetry**: docking pay costs the boss almost nothing (one signature); resisting costs the worker enormously (arbitration, unemployment risk). Every worker-side theory answers one question: **how do you rebuild cost symmetry without united-front value?**

## Five theoretical tools

**1. Exit, Voice, Loyalty (Hirschman)**
- Exit (job change): the highest-weighted option — labor-market mobility is the source of power;
- Voice: negotiation < written objection < labor-inspector complaint < labor arbitration < lawsuit, in increasing cost;
- Loyalty (endurance): rational only when improvement is expected or while accumulating capital for exit;
- The three are cost calculations, not personality choices, and combine: "outward loyalty + quiet exit preparation + preserved voice evidence."

**2. Labor law as rule terrain**
- Statutory law naturally sides with the worker: withheld wages, bonus deductions without a policy basis, and fines are almost unambiguously illegal;
- In wage disputes the burden of proof is reversed (the employer must prove) — directly inverting the cost asymmetry;
- Labor arbitration is free with a one-year limitation period; activation conditions: **evidence** (contract, pay stubs, attendance, deduction notices, chat logs) + **timing** (complaining while employed risks retaliation; concentrating claims at the exit window is often optimal).

**3. Logic of collective action (Olson)**
- Exploitation is often designed to divide and conquer (separate talks, differentiated deductions, pay secrecy) to sustain the free-rider dilemma;
- Counter: small-circle information linking (3~5 core trusted people comparing notes and sharing evidence), via legal channels (collective consultation, union, collective arbitration). Small groups beat large mobilization at overcoming free-riding.

**4. Credible commitment and reputation mechanisms**
- The credibility of a resistance threat depends on evidence, procedural fluency, and unemployment tolerance;
- **Your reaction to the first infringement sets the baseline for every later round**: respond to the first small infringement with a low-cost written boundary ("please provide the basis for this deduction") — lowest cost, strongest signal.

**5. Human-capital theory — the ultimate exit right**
- Distinguish general human capital (skills, certificates, industry reputation) from firm-specific capital (experience valuable only at the current company);
- The boss's confidence in squeezing you comes from your over-specialization. Counter: continuously convert labor into general capital (certifications, portable work products, industry contacts, keep interview touch every year);
- People who know their market price and people who don't have different micro-expressions at the negotiation table.

## Worker strategy order

```
Layer 1 (continuous): generalize human capital — able to leave anytime, leaving profitably
Layer 2 (daily): written boundaries at the first infringement — build a "not to be messed with" reputation
Layer 3 (mid-game): full-cycle paper trail — contract, pay stubs, attendance, deduction records
Layer 4 (optional): small-circle information linking — break the divide-and-conquer
Layer 5 (timing): concentrated claims at the exit window — inspector complaint / arbitration, burden of proof on them
```

**Distinguish two battlefields**: illegal exploitation (wage arrears, deductions) goes to rule terrain — the law wins steadily; "legal but awful" management (strict reviews, low pay) can only be met with exit capability and collective bargaining — don't waste ammunition on unwinnable battlefields.

Worker master line: **For those without united-front value, the rules are your united-front value and exit rights are your bargaining chips. Make every squeeze troublesome, documented, and capable of triggering legal process — and the other side will naturally go pick a softer target.**

# Part 3: Universal discipline and output

## Card sequencing (both types)

1. **Compliance first**: before negotiating, make sure the user is unimpeachable — the more cards you hold, the cleaner you must be;
2. **Internal rule cards before external-force cards**: speak with the counterpart's own documents / national law; external force stays in hand as background deterrence, 0% spent on formal complaints, unless fundamental interests are touched (nuclear option: one decisive strike, no repeated tugging);
3. **A threat's credibility is worth more than its use**: a played card depreciates; held in hand it deters continuously;
4. **Hole cards and evidence chains only need to be faintly known to exist** — contents stay until the end;
5. **No frontal conflict before the evidence chain is formed**: cooperative posture + written trails, not confrontation.

## Output format

Default output is a strategy analysis document:

1. User classification and counterpart-type diagnosis
2. Overall situation assessment (problem characterization table, counterpart's behavioral logic, power position)
3. Strategy map
4. Concrete tactics per module/layer (sample scripts, written templates, likely counterpart reactions and counters)
5. Action timeline (week-by-week, checklist)
6. Risk warnings and self-checks (rule texts and contract clauses to verify)
7. One-line master principle

Script style: the user's internal scripts always take a "report + ask for advice" posture, interests first, inviting the counterpart to comment and absorb — people don't veto things they participated in.

## Boundaries and reminders

- Provide no illegal or rule-breaking means; for the user's gray-zone operations, help build a "defensible narrative" and mark red lines clearly, without encouraging expansion;
- Where specific policies and laws are involved (establishment discipline, labor-law details, local regulations), remind the user to verify the original text; search to confirm when necessary;
- The purpose of the paper trail is facts for negotiation and self-protection in the worst case — not burning the relationship.
